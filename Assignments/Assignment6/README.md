# 🌍 Assignment 6: Social Determinants of Health (SDOH) & AI

## 📋 Overview

In this assignment, I explored how **Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)** — the non-medical factors that influence health outcomes — affect patient well-being, and how artificial intelligence can help healthcare systems identify and address these social needs. I focused on three critical SDOH factors: **food insecurity, housing instability, and education access**.

---

## 🧪 Methods

I analyzed each SDOH factor by examining:

- The **health impact** of each social determinant on patient populations.
- Current **screening gaps** in clinical settings that fail to capture SDOH data.
- **AI-driven interventions** that could help identify at-risk patients and connect them with community resources.

---

## 📊 Results

### SDOH Factor Analysis

| SDOH Factor | Health Impact | AI Intervention |
|-------------|--------------|------------------|
| **Food Insecurity** | Increased rates of diabetes, obesity, cardiovascular disease, and malnutrition | NLP-based screening tools that analyze clinical notes to flag food insecurity; predictive models using claims and demographic data |
| **Housing Instability** | Higher rates of mental illness, substance abuse, infectious disease; frequent ER visits | ML models that predict housing instability from EHR data; geospatial AI mapping patients to housing resources |
| **Education Access** | Lower health literacy leading to poor medication adherence and missed preventive care | AI-powered chatbots delivering health education at appropriate literacy levels; personalized patient education platforms |

### Impact by the Numbers

| Statistic | Source |
|-----------|--------|
| 1 in 8 Americans experienced food insecurity in 2023 | USDA |
| Homeless individuals have life expectancy 17 years shorter than housed populations | NHCHC |
| Adults with low health literacy are 1.5–3x more likely to experience adverse outcomes | AHRQ |

---

## 💬 Discussion

Social determinants account for an estimated **30–55% of health outcomes**, yet most healthcare systems focus almost exclusively on clinical interventions. AI has the potential to bridge this gap by automating SDOH screening through NLP, predicting risk before a crisis occurs, and connecting patients to resources through intelligent referral systems.

However, AI-driven SDOH interventions must be deployed carefully to avoid reinforcing existing biases.

---

## 💡 What I Learned

- **Healthcare outcomes depend heavily on non-clinical factors**; treating disease without addressing social needs is often ineffective.
- **AI can help screen for SDOH at scale**, but must be combined with strong community health infrastructure.
- **Health equity requires intentional design** — AI tools must be validated with diverse populations.
- **Interdisciplinary collaboration** between clinicians, social workers, and data scientists is essential.
